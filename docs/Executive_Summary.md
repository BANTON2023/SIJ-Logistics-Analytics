# SIJ Logistics & Analytics — Executive Summary and Recommendations

**Period covered:** January 2022 to December 2024 (three years)
**Prepared by:** Mbaji Austin
**Source:** SIJ Logistics & Analytics Power BI report (`TSA_capstone.pbip`) and the underlying
operating data: loads, trips, deliveries, fuel, maintenance and safety records.

---

## The short version

SIJ Logistics is a busy, steady business that is **not keeping its delivery promises**.

- It earned **$262.5 million** from **85,410 loads** for **200 customers**, and revenue
  was flat at about $87–88 million a year.
- **Only 56% of pickups and deliveries arrive on time.** Deliveries do worst: fewer than
  half (45%) are on time.
- Drivers lose a lot of time **waiting at customer docks** (92 minutes per stop on average),
  and trucks spend **28% of trip time idling**.
- **Fuel is 92% of the costs we can measure.** Cost per load fell 12% over the period, but
  mostly because diesel became cheaper, not because the fleet became more efficient.

**The top priority is reliable delivery times.** It is the clearest weakness, it affects
every customer, and it is mostly a planning problem that can be fixed without buying anything.

## What we found

### 1. Customers: steady, spread out, with room to grow

- **Low dependence on any single customer.** The 10 largest customers bring in only 5.7%
  of revenue. Losing one account would not hurt the business badly.
- **Revenue is evenly split** between contract (38%), spot (32%) and dedicated (31%)
  customers, and between refrigerated and dry-van loads (50/50). Average revenue per load
  is about $3,070 for every customer type and freight type, so no segment is clearly
  more valuable per load than another.
- **Big untapped potential.** Customers' stated annual freight budgets add up to
  $538 million a year. We earned about $87.5 million a year, roughly 16% of that.
  36 customers give us less than 10% of their potential.
- **Inactive accounts still matter.** 32 customers are marked inactive, yet over the
  period they produced $42.6 million (16% of revenue).

### 2. Service: the clearest problem

- **Only 56% of stops are on time**: 67% of pickups but just 45% of deliveries.
- **Late stops are badly late.** A typical late stop is about 3 hours behind schedule.
- **Schedules do not match reality.** Trips actually take about 1 day, against 2.1 days
  planned, and 9,382 stops were marked late because they arrived **more than 2 hours
  early**. This points to appointment times being set without using real drive times.
- **The problem is the same everywhere.** On-time rates are 55–56% at terminals,
  warehouses, cross-docks and distribution centres alike, and every year from 2022 to 2024.
  This is a company-wide planning issue, not a handful of bad locations.
- **Long waits at docks.** The average wait is 92 minutes. 61,785 stops (36%) waited
  more than 2 hours, which adds up to 49,800 hours of waiting beyond a 2-hour allowance.
  Only $6.1 million in extra charges was billed over three years.

### 3. Fleet: well used, but wasting fuel

- **Trucks are busy.** Average utilization is 83%, steady across the three years.
- **Idling is high.** Engines idled for about 599,000 hours, 28% of all trip time.
- **Fuel economy is flat** at 6.45 MPG for every truck make, so savings will come from how
  trucks are driven and parked, not from switching brands.
- **Maintenance is reactive.** About 1 in 3 maintenance jobs (971 of 2,920) is
  described as an emergency. 2018 model-year trucks cost the most to maintain
  ($0.073 per mile, more than twice the $0.032 of 2020 trucks).

### 4. Cost: fuel drives almost everything

- **Fuel is 92% of tracked operating cost** ($93.1M of $101.3M).
- **Cost per load fell 12%**, from $1,272 in 2022 to $1,114 in 2024. Over the same period
  diesel fell from $4.20 to $3.65 a gallon. The improvement came from the market, not from
  efficiency, and would reverse if fuel prices rise again.
- **Where we buy fuel barely matters.** Average prices differ by only about 2 cents a
  gallon between the cheapest and most expensive states. Savings must come from **using
  less fuel**, not from buying it elsewhere.

### 5. Safety

- 170 incidents, 33 with injuries, and $2.65 million in claims.
- 38% of incidents were preventable, accounting for $0.9 million in claims.
- **Risk is concentrated.** 18 drivers had 3 or more incidents, and two drivers had 6 and 7.

## Recommendations

In order of priority. Estimates are rough and meant to show scale.

| # | Action | Why | Expected benefit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Fix delivery appointment planning.** Set appointment times from actual drive times, confirm them with the receiver, and review on-time performance weekly by customer. | Only 45% of deliveries are on time, and many "late" stops actually arrived hours early. | Better customer retention and a stronger case for rate increases. Target: 75% on time within 12 months. |
| 2 | **Charge for waiting time.** Agree a 2-hour free allowance with customers and bill detention after that. | 49,800 hours of waiting beyond 2 hours, but only $6.1M of extra charges billed in three years. | About **$3.7M** over three years at $75 an hour, and an incentive for customers to unload faster. |
| 3 | **Reduce engine idling.** Set idle limits, coach drivers using the idle data, and consider cab heaters/coolers that work with the engine off. | Engines idle 28% of trip time. | Halving idle time saves roughly 240,000 gallons, about **$0.9M** over three years.* |
| 4 | **Coach high-risk drivers.** Start with the 18 drivers who had 3 or more incidents. | Risk is concentrated, and 38% of incidents were preventable. | Fewer injuries and part of the **$0.9M** in preventable claims. |
| 5 | **Grow existing accounts and win back lost ones.** Contact the 32 inactive customers, and give sales a target list of the 36 customers using us for under 10% of their freight. | We capture only 16% of customers' stated potential. | Growing capture from 16% to 20% would add about **$20M a year** in revenue. |
| 6 | **Move from emergency to planned maintenance**, and review the 2018 model-year trucks for replacement. | 1 in 3 jobs is an emergency, and the 2018 trucks cost the most per mile. | Less unplanned downtime and fewer roadside breakdowns. |
| 7 | **Complete the data.** Add driver pay, insurance and overheads, and record a truck for every trip. | Profit margins (61%) are overstated without these costs, and 1,672 trips (2%) have no truck recorded. | A true profit figure and reliable cost per truck. |

\* Assumes a parked truck burns about 0.8 gallons an hour at $3.90 a gallon.

---

## Things to keep in mind

- **Profit is overstated.** The data only includes fuel and maintenance costs. With driver
  wages, insurance and overheads, real margins will be much lower than the 61% shown.
- **Customer and route margins use fuel only.** Maintenance is recorded per truck, not per
  load, so "Margin after Fuel" is the fair way to compare customers and routes.
- **The Summary & Actions page uses fixed figures** for the full three years. The other
  report pages are interactive and respond to the filters.
